# Week 2 — Define & Ideate: From problem to ideas

*Minggu 2 — Mendefinisikan & Mencari Ide: Dari masalah ke ide*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Design thinking stage** | Define, then Ideate |
| **English focus** | Problem statements, "How might we…?", modals (could / should / might / must), comparatives and superlatives |
| **Prompting focus** | **A**ction verbs, **F**ormat, **T**one — the full CRAFT; AI as a brainstorming partner |
| **Socratic focus** | Assumptions and viewpoints |
| **App** | Project Studio → *Define* and *Ideate* canvases → **Generate brief** · Prompt Lab |
| **Output** | Project brief (one page, English, with Indonesian translation) |

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this week, students can:

- Write a clear problem statement and a "How might we…?" question in English
- Use modals to suggest and compare ideas (*We could… · It might… · We should…*)
- Write a complete CRAFT prompt with a precise action verb, output format and tone
- Use AI to generate many ideas, then judge them with clear criteria — instead of accepting the first answer

## Language focus

### 1. Problem statement and "How might we…?"

**Pattern:** *[User] needs [a way to …] because [insight].*

> *First-year students in Surabaya need a way to practise English speaking without fear, because they told us they feel embarrassed in class.*

**How might we…?** turns a problem into an opportunity:

> *How might we help first-year students practise speaking English without feeling embarrassed?*

✅ Not too wide (*"How might we improve education?"*) · ✅ Not a solution in disguise (*"How might we build an app that…"*)

### 2. Modals for ideas

| Modal | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **could** | possibility, an option | *We **could** make a WhatsApp chatbot.* |
| **might** | less certain | *Users **might** not trust AI answers.* |
| **should** | recommendation | *We **should** test with real sellers first.* |
| **must** | strong rule (also in prompts!) | *The answer **must** be under 100 words.* |

### 3. Comparing ideas

*Idea A is **cheaper than** idea B. · Idea C is **the most feasible**. · Idea B is **not as useful as** idea A.*

### 4. Action verbs for prompts

Weak: *make, do, help, tell me about* → Strong: **list, summarise, compare, explain, rewrite, translate, classify, design, generate, evaluate, draft**

## Session plans

### Session 1 (2 h): Define — what is the real problem?

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 15 min | Warm-up: teams read their 3 insights aloud. Others ask one *clarification* question. |
| 25 min | **Language focus:** problem statements and HMW. Fix 6 weak examples together. |
| 35 min | Teams write 3 versions of their problem statement in the **Define canvas**, then chat with the coach. Expect *assumption* questions: *"What are you assuming about your users?"* |
| 25 min | **Viewpoints carousel:** each team posts their HMW; other teams add sticky notes: *"From a [teacher/parent/seller]'s view, …"* |
| 20 min | Teams choose their final HMW and define what success looks like. |

### Session 2 (2 h): Ideate with (and without) AI

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 15 min | **Crazy 8s** (no AI!): 8 ideas in 8 minutes, sketched on paper. Human ideas first. |
| 20 min | **Language focus:** full CRAFT — Action, Format, Tone. Action verb drill. |
| 35 min | **AI brainstorm.** Teams write a brainstorm prompt in Indonesian → Prompt Lab → choose the Structured or Advanced option → run it in an AI tool. Target: 20+ ideas in total. Prompt hint: *"Give me 15 ideas… Include unusual ideas… Use a table with columns: idea, who it helps, effort (low/medium/high)."* |
| 30 min | **Choose with criteria.** Impact/effort matrix. Teams use modals and comparatives to discuss: *"Idea 4 could… but it is more difficult than…"* |
| 20 min | Fill in the **Ideate canvas**. Coach asks *implications* questions: *"If you build this, what could go wrong?"* |

### Session 3 (2 h): The project brief

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 20 min | Teams finish the Define + Ideate canvases. |
| 15 min | **Generate brief** in Project Studio. Read the *Open questions* section — these are gaps to fix. |
| 30 min | **Fix the gaps:** answer the open questions with the coach, update the canvas, regenerate. Students must check that the brief says only what *they* decided. |
| 35 min | **Gallery walk:** briefs on screens; peers give feedback with: *"I like… · I wonder… · What if…?"* |
| 20 min | Each team takes the **first prompt idea** from the brief to the Prompt Lab and saves one improved prompt. |

## Homework

1. Show your HMW and chosen idea to **one real user** from Week 1. Write down their reaction in English.
2. Save **2 more prompts** to your Portfolio (one brainstorm, one research) with reflections.

## Deliverable checklist

- [ ] Define and Ideate canvases complete
- [ ] Project brief generated and checked by the team
- [ ] Idea list (20+) and impact/effort matrix photo
- [ ] 4 prompts total in the Portfolio

## Teacher notes

- **Watch for "AI picked our idea".** The decision must be the team's, using criteria. Ask: *"Why this one and not idea 7?"* — the answer must be in English.
- **The "direct translation vs. expanded prompt" contrast is the core lesson.** Have students circle every word in the Structured option that was *not* in their draft, and ask: is this assumption correct? Square-bracket words are the Lab's guesses.
- Feasibility check: the prototype must be buildable in **one week without coding** (or with AI-written code for students who can run it).
